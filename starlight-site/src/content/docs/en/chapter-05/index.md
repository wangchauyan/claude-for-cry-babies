Welcome to the chapter you'll probably bookmark. Not because you're bad at this -- but because **everyone** ends up here. Even seasoned developers spend roughly 40% of their time debugging. The difference is they've learned not to take it personally.

Here's the secret that separates people who build things from people who give up: **errors aren't failures. They're conversations.** Every error message is Claude tapping you on the shoulder saying, "Hey, I tried my best with what you gave me, but I'm confused about something. Can we talk?"

Think of it like cooking. You followed a recipe, and the souffl collapsed. You didn't fail at cooking. The oven was too hot, or you opened the door too early, or Mercury was in retrograde. The point is: you identify what went wrong, adjust, and try again. The souffl doesn't judge you. Neither does Claude.

In this chapter, you'll learn to:
- Recognize the three main types of errors (without needing a CS degree)
- Apply a universal three-step fix that works 80% of the time
- Walk through real error scenarios with copy-paste fix prompts
- Use "Chain of Thought" to prevent errors before they happen
- Know when to gracefully admit defeat and start fresh (it's a superpower, not a failure)
