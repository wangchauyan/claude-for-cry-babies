## The Real Change Isn't Technical

Look at that table again. The left column isn't about lacking skills. It's about a **mindset**: "This isn't for me. I'm not that kind of person. I need someone else to do this."

The right column isn't about having skills either. It's about a different mindset: "I can figure this out. Let me try. I have tools that can help."

The real transformation of this book isn't that you learned to write prompts or build Artifacts. It's that you shifted from:

**"Who can help me?"** --> **"How can I solve this?"**

That's not a tool switch. That's an identity shift. And identity shifts are the changes that actually stick.

You're not just "someone who can use AI." You're someone who **thinks like a builder.** That's a much bigger deal.

## A Note About Imposter Syndrome

Right about now, a voice in your head might be saying: "But I'm not a *real* developer. I'm just using AI to do the work."

Let's address that directly: **so what?**

A "real" photographer uses Photoshop. A "real" architect uses CAD software. A "real" writer uses spell-check, grammar tools, and research databases. Nobody questions their legitimacy for using tools that make them more effective.

You're using the most powerful tool of this decade to solve real problems and build real things. The tool doesn't diminish your work. Your judgment, your problem identification, your ability to describe what's needed, your iterative refinement -- those are all *yours*.

You're not a fake builder using real tools. You're a real builder using smart tools.
